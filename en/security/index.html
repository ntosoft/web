SECURITY

Security and data protection

Healthcare is one of the sectors covered by the new Czech Cybersecurity Act. We therefore address security not only technically but also in a way that lets the facility demonstrate its compliance: from risk and supply chain management to incident reporting and recovery.

Encryption

TLS 1.2 and 1.3 for all data in transit, mutual certificate authentication with national services and AES-256 encryption of stored data.

Identity and access

Role-based access control, multi-factor authentication and single sign-on (SSO) through OpenID Connect.

Audit trail

A tamper-evident record of who accessed which data, and when.

Backups and recovery

Regular encrypted backups, routinely tested restores and a business continuity plan.

Secure development

Code review, automated tests, dependency scanning and regular security testing.

Privacy by design

Data minimization, pseudonymization and tooling to honor data subject rights under GDPR.

LEGISLATION

The Cybersecurity Act and healthcare

Since 1 November 2025 the Czech Republic has a new Act No. 264/2025 Coll. on cybersecurity, which transposes the EU NIS2 directive and replaces the original act from 2014. The supervisory authority is the National Cyber and Information Security Agency (NÚKIB).

The act works with the concept of a regulated service and places providers in either a higher or a lower obligations regime. Healthcare is listed among the regulated services, so the act also applies to some healthcare providers. Whether and in which regime it applies to a specific facility is determined by the act and the decree on regulated services. For a first orientation NÚKIB offers an online calculator on its portal.

RegulationWhat it coversWhat it means for software

SUPPORTING COMPLIANCE

How we help facilities meet the requirements

Under the act, accountability for cybersecurity lies with the facility's management. As a supplier we are part of its supply chain, so we deliver not only software but also the documentation and cooperation the facility needs to meet its obligations.

Assets and risks

A description of system components, data flows and interfaces as input for the facility's asset register and risk analysis, together with the threats considered during design and the measures that address them.

Supply chain

Security requirements in the contract, a list of libraries and components used (SBOM), information on subcontractors and hosting location, and rules for supplier remote access to the facility's environment.

Incident detection and reporting

Security events from the system can be forwarded to the facility's tools. In an incident we provide prompt cooperation and evidence so the facility can report within the statutory deadlines. In the higher regime the initial report is due within 24 hours.

Continuity and recovery

Agreed recovery objectives, i.e. how long the system may be down and how much data may be lost. The recovery procedure is documented and regularly tested. Fallback procedures exist for the most critical activities, such as patient admission or issuing a prescription.

Data location and operation

Operation in the facility's infrastructure or in the cloud with data stored in the European Union. The exact location and administrators are known and documented, which makes assessing data localisation requirements easier.

Documentation for audits

System security documentation, a description of roles and permissions, logging and backup settings and update records. The facility can use them directly in its own security documentation and during audits.

DETAILS

Certificate management

Private keys and certificates used to communicate with national services are stored in an encrypted store with restricted access. Validity is monitored automatically and administrators are warned well ahead of expiry so a renewal never causes a communication outage.

Audit trail and activity journal

Every operation on patient data is recorded: who performed it, when, in which system, for what purpose and with what result. Records are protected against tampering, retained for the period required by regulation and can be forwarded to a security information and event management (SIEM) system via syslog.

SECURE DEVELOPMENT

Security across the whole lifecycle

  1. Design

    Threat modelling for every new interface and data flow.

  2. Development

    Code review by a second person, secure defaults, no secrets in code.

  3. Checks

    Automated static analysis and checks for known vulnerabilities in libraries.

  4. Testing

    Interface security tests and a penetration test before major releases.

  5. Release

    Signed packages, a component list and a record of what changed.

  6. Operation

    Monitoring new vulnerabilities and releasing patches by severity.

Incidents and business continuity

For every deployment we have a documented procedure for security incidents and outages: who is informed, how impact is contained, how operation is restored and how the event is reviewed. Backups are regularly verified by restoring them, and fallback procedures are ready for key functions such as issuing a prescription or admitting a patient.
